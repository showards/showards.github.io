    About Me

    I am passionate about data and its storage; data for machine learning and business intelligence.

    My career in technology started as an adolescent volunteering for the local library helping administer its bulletin board system. As I got older I moved to a local ISP which exposed me to full-blown system administration, and later, database administration. The death of a company owner allowed my inner-engineer to flourish with the deconstruction of the accounting database’s relational logic.

    My current interest, at the time, shifted more toward social sciences and helping individuals. An in-home care and facilitator role connected me with some amazing advocates whose efforts included presentations for the budget committees of the California State Legislature and participation in various statewide committees and commissions. The experience fostered an admiration and respect for the deliberative process, the law, and the courts.

    While I worked as a facilitator, I continued with technological projects which included a short-lived internet show, small retailer projects, and a farm-to-chef market project. The goal of the farm-to-chef market project was the connection of farms, through a farmer’s market, to both restaurants and consumers. It was realized the relationships were analogous to a supply chain.

    I started at The Home Depot to learn the retail business from the one of the best, and looking toward a career in data engineering. I focused heavily on the processes of the business and looked for both: reductions in shrink and efficiency gains. There were opportunities to communicate about the logistics of loss from the overheads. I voiced concerns and offered constructive comments when the opportunity arose. Many of the suggestions and comments, including exact wording, were incorporated into the software associates use on a daily basis to manage overhead spaces.

    There was a time when the software that handled customer agreements was being phased out for a more viable product. As the decommission time neared, it became crunch time for new software in mid-2023, as the old software was to be disabled at the end of the year. There was zero indication, from a sales specialist’s perspective, the necessary data structures were in development to ensure a smooth transition.

    I spent a few hours and put together a relational model diagram, for the provision of service providers and services, provided it as feedback. The diagram was built from insight gained during the farm-to-chef project: supply chains and the management of deliveries to restaurants and customers. Features of the submitted diagram were slowly implemented over the next two years.

    Over a life, I have learned the importance of adding value where deep understanding exists. I have had the opportunity to maintain or create tremendous value for others twice in my life, and I believe they were both successes. It is time to do it again.
